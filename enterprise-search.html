In this case study

The opportunity

JPMC’s intranet search was unreliable. Employees struggled to find the information, tools and services they needed, often relying on colleagues rather than resolving tasks independently.

The opportunity was to make search a dependable front door to the organisation—helping employees move from a question to the right answer, person or workflow with minimal effort.

It also created an opportunity to unify fragmented search experiences across the phonebook, SharePoint and Confluence. Refreshing employee profiles, which had seen little development for more than a decade despite being among the intranet’s most-used destinations, was central to that ambition.

My role

I led product design for a unified enterprise-search experience spanning the intranet, people directory and key knowledge sources. I conducted the discovery work, combining competitive analysis with a deep exploration of modern search practices—particularly relevance, AI and machine learning, and the principles of an intuitive, low-effort experience.

The work brought previously separate search experiences and content ecosystems into a coherent product direction. I shaped an experience strategy centred on relevance, actionability and speed, using AI and machine learning to improve results over time rather than treating search as a static list of links.

A further priority was reducing the effort required to complete everyday tasks. Employees previously had to navigate multiple, often tedious steps to reach the workflows, systems and processes they relied on. The new experience streamlined these journeys, enabling people to get to what they needed in one or two clicks.

The product transformation

The new search experience was designed to help employees complete common tasks in three clicks or fewer. It introduced:

  • An AI/ML-powered search backend to improve relevance over the legacy experience
  • Unified search across intranet content, people, Confluence and SharePoint
  • Featured results that took employees directly into complex workflows
  • A reimagined people directory, with richer employee profiles and organisational charts integrated into search
  • In-product relevance feedback, allowing employees to help improve results over time
  • AI-suggested answers that surfaced useful extracts from help documentation

The result was a more useful, action-oriented search experience—one that helped employees find information, connect with the right people and take the next step without unnecessary navigation.

Impact

Since launch, the experience has demonstrated strong adoption and engagement:

4mSearches made
239kUnique users
70%Search click-through rate
61%Searches resulting in a click on a top result

Leadership takeaway: This was not a search-interface refresh. It was a product transformation: establishing a unified search strategy across fragmented enterprise systems and directing an experience that made a complex organisation easier to navigate.
